Were the Lights Too Bright for African Teams?

A football analytics project exploring pressure, late-match performance, tactical risk, opponent strength, and squad depth at the 2026 FIFA World Cup.

Were the lights too bright for African teams — or does the data tell a different story?

Overview

A late defeat does not necessarily mean a team choked.

This project investigates whether apparent late-match collapses among African teams represent:

* Sudden performance decline
* Sustained attrition
* Tactical retreat
* Calculated risk that ultimately failed
* Post-error cascades
* Isolated decisive events

The analysis separates strategy, execution, and outcome rather than judging performance from the final score alone.

Key Analysis

* Pre- vs post-80th-minute performance
* Rolling Field Tilt
* Cumulative xG conceded
* Passing and possession retention
* Shots and chances conceded
* Score state
* FIFA ranking and opponent-strength context
* Tactical approach and territorial control
* Bench-depth disparity
* Substitution impact
* African teams vs comparable non-African teams
* South Africa’s three-match case study

Match Story

The Match Story timeline combines:

* Cumulative xG conceded
* Rolling Field Tilt
* Goals
* Substitutions
* Cards
* Match events

This helps distinguish a sudden late collapse from a match where pressure accumulated steadily from the beginning.

South Africa Case Study

A focused analysis of South Africa’s three World Cup matches examining:

Strategy → Execution → Outcome

Each match is considered in the context of the opponent’s FIFA ranking, territorial control, attacking threat, defensive approach, and late-match performance.
